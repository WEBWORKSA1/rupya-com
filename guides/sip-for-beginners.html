SIP for beginners: how monthly investing works

Updated November 2026 · 4 min read

A systematic investment plan is not a product. It is an instruction: move a fixed amount from my bank account into a mutual fund on the same date every month.

Why it works

Your fixed amount buys more units when prices are low and fewer when they are high. Over time this evens out your purchase price, an effect called rupee-cost averaging. The larger benefit is behavioural. The money leaves before you can spend it and you do not need to decide each month whether the market looks expensive.

Compounding does the rest. ₹10,000 a month at 12% a year grows to about ₹23 lakh in 10 years and about ₹1 crore in 20. The second decade adds more than three times the first because growth is earned on earlier growth.

Starting in five steps

  1. Complete KYC once with PAN, Aadhaar and a bank account. It is done online in minutes.
  2. Pick the goal and time frame. Equity funds suit goals at least five years away. For shorter goals use debt funds or deposits.
  3. Choose a fund. A low-cost index fund tracking a broad index is a sound first choice because it needs no forecasting of which manager will do well.
  4. Choose the direct plan. Every fund has a regular plan, which pays a distributor commission from your money, and a direct plan, which does not. The difference of around half to one percentage point a year compounds into lakhs.
  5. Set up the auto-debit for a date soon after your salary arrives.

Mistakes that cost the most

  • Stopping when markets fall. Falls are when a SIP buys cheapest. Stopping then locks in the worst of both worlds.
  • Chasing last year’s best fund. Top performers rotate. Costs and consistency predict more than recent returns.
  • Owning too many funds. Five funds that hold the same large companies are one fund with five statements.
  • Never raising the amount. A 10% annual step-up roughly doubles the final corpus over 20 years.

What a SIP cannot do

It cannot remove risk. An equity SIP can show a loss for years, particularly early on. Keep an emergency fund first so you are never forced to sell at a bad time.
