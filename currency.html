How to read these rates

The figures above are mid-market reference rates: the midpoint between the prices at which banks buy and sell a currency. Nobody exchanges money for you at exactly this rate. A bank, card network, airport counter or transfer app quotes a rate with a margin built in and may add a fee on top. The mid-market rate is the yardstick for judging those quotes. If the reference rate is ₹88 to a dollar and you are offered ₹86.50, the margin is about 1.7%.

Why the rupee moves

The rupee floats, so its price is set by demand and supply. Demand for dollars rises when India imports more, especially crude oil, and when foreign investors sell Indian assets. Supply rises with exports, remittances from Indians abroad and foreign investment. Interest-rate differences and global risk appetite shift these flows. The Reserve Bank of India does not fix a level but buys and sells dollars to smooth sharp swings.

How often are the rates updated?

The reference rates refresh once a day. A daily job also stores a copy on this site so the page works if the live source is unreachable.

How do lakh and crore map to millions?

One lakh is 1,00,000 (a hundred thousand). One crore is 1,00,00,000 (ten million). One million is ten lakh and one billion is a hundred crore.
